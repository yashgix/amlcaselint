# examcheck

Failures FinCEN cited in bank enforcement orders, turned into deterministic checks over AML alert-case logs.

Write-up: https://yashgix.github.io/examcheck/

## Why

The question: **what did FinCEN actually penalize banks for, in the regulator's own words, and which of those failures can be turned into deterministic checks over an alert-case log?**

Three parts, all offline with no model calls:

1. A corpus of FinCEN's public enforcement actions against depository institutions, pinned in `data/`, with a keyword baseline that finds cited failures in ten fixed categories. Every finding is a sentence of an order, so every claim points to the regulator's text.
2. The baseline scored against a gold set of 15 orders labelled from the pinned text, where every label carries an exact quote.
3. Four checks over a case log: late SAR, aged alert, closure without rationale or evidence, and closure only because a SAR or CTR was filed before. Each check is tied to quoted passages. **No real AML alert data is public**, so the checks run on a clearly synthetic log whose sizes and delays are taken from figures in the orders. Rates measured on it describe the checks, never any bank.

## Data and licenses

All files under `data/` are fixed and never regenerated by the code. `data/MANIFEST.json` lists the sha256 and size of every other file in `data/`; run `tools/fetch_data.py --verify` to check the pinned files without network access. `tools/fetch_data.py` can also re-download the PDFs and re-extract text with `pdftotext -enc UTF-8` (Poppler; the pinned text came from version 26.09.0). A different Poppler build may extract slightly different text, so the pinned text remains the reference.

The FinCEN orders and 31 CFR 1020.320 are works of the US federal government, which carry no US copyright (17 U.S.C. 105). FinCEN's site policy, quoted in `data/LICENSE.md`, calls the site public information that may be distributed or copied, with credit requested. Credit: Financial Crimes Enforcement Network, US Department of the Treasury. The text extracts under `data/orders/` are unedited derivatives of those PDFs. The code is MIT (`LICENSE`). The labels in `data/gold.json` and `data/gold-v2.json` are this project's own work and fall under the same MIT license. This is a reading of the published terms, not legal advice.

- `data/orders.json`: the 50 actions FinCEN tags Depository Institutions, fetched 14 Nov 2026, in site order. Each entry has the id, date, title, number, PDF URL, PDF hash and size, page count, status and text path. `status` is `included` (39), `no_text_layer` (8 scanned PDFs with no text) or `individual` (3 actions against people, not institutions).
- `data/orders/<id>.txt`: unedited pdftotext output. Older orders are OCR'd scans and may have character errors; quotes are matched against the text exactly as it is pinned.
- `data/regulation/31-cfr-1020.320.xml`: the eCFR XML of 31 CFR 1020.320, fetched 14 Nov 2026.
- `data/gold.json`: labels for 15 orders (v1, frozen). The labeller is Claude (Opus 5.5), 14 Nov 2026, working from the pinned text, and Yash Gupta has not yet spot-checked the labels. A category absent from an order is a negative label. The baseline patterns were frozen before labelling. The full method is in the file.
- `data/gold-v2.json`: the same labels plus one, Lone Star (18845) `independent_testing`, which an independent review proposed. Yash Gupta checked it on 15 Nov 2026 and rejected it. The sentence is in section III of the order, which summarises the OCC's own earlier action, not in FinCEN's Determinations. So the v1 label (negative) stands, and the file is kept only as the record of the rejected proposal. It is not in `MANIFEST.json`, which lists the pinned v1 files.
- Labelling rule: Labels count only what FinCEN states in its own findings section of each order ("Determinations", or "Statement of Facts" in newer orders). A sentence there may cite another regulator's examination. A separate section that summarises another regulator's own enforcement action is not counted. All 98 v1 positive quotes come from those sections.
- `examcheck.DATA_DIR` points at the repository `data/` directory.

## Synthetic alert log

No real AML alert data is public. The alert log produced by `examcheck/synth.py` is therefore synthetic: sizes and delays are taken from figures quoted in the pinned orders (`SOURCES`), and the near-miss mix is an assumption chosen to test each check's boundary. Rates measured on this generated log describe the checks, never any bank.

## Run

All commands use Python 3.14 with the standard library unless stated.

```bash
make test
python experiment.py --out results/metrics.json
python demo.py
python demo.py --readme README.md --out PATH
python -m examcheck findings
python -m examcheck findings --order 19518
python -m examcheck synth --out results/synthetic.json
python -m examcheck check results/synthetic.json --as-of 2021-03-29
python -m examcheck check results/synthetic.json --as-of 2021-03-29 --naive
python -m examcheck check results/synthetic.json --as-of 2021-03-29 --cannot-assess
python -m examcheck review results/synthetic.json --as-of 2021-03-29
python -m examcheck failures --out results/failures.json
```

The experiment is deterministic and takes about a second. Two runs produce byte-identical metrics.

### Basis

**`late_sar`**

- 31 CFR 1020.320: "A bank is required to file a SAR no later than 30 calendar days after the date of initial detection by the bank of facts that may constitute a basis for filing a SAR."
- 31 CFR 1020.320: "In no case shall reporting be delayed more than 60 calendar days after the date of initial detection of a reportable transaction."
- Shinhan Bank America: "the Bank filed several hundred SARs an average of approximately 119 days late"
- Beach Bank: "the Bank filed 75 initial suspicious activity reports, 67 of which were not timely filed."

**`aged_alert`**

- Gibraltar Private Bank and Trust Company: "Gibraltar failed to review and close or escalate nearly 60% of its monthly alerts in the 30 days prescribed by Gibraltar’s own BSA/AML policy."
- Shinhan Bank America: "At one point, the Bank had a backlog of over 10,000 alert events (within more than 700 pre-cases) that had not been reviewed."
- TD Bank, N.A. and TD Bank USA, N.A.: "In 2018, the AML program registered over 70,000 backlogged detection alerts and roughly 3,000 aged subpoena responses and further investigation cases, with less than 60% of alerts falling within benchmarks for timely detection."
- Lone Star National Bank: "Lone Star had 4,888 outstanding alerts, 627 cases, and 213 internal referrals."

**`no_rationale`**

- Shinhan Bank America: "the Bank’s internal policies required analysts to consider and append documentation on any alerts raised by the TM system."
- Korea Exchange Bank, New York, New York: "the Branch could not produce any analysis or documentation to support the U.S. Compliance Officer’s assertion that the cash activity appeared normal."
- CommunityBank of Texas, N.A.: "did not provide sufficient support to justify the closure."

**`prior_filing_only`**

- Shinhan Bank America: "At times, the justification for clearing an alert, as opposed to escalating it, relied on whether the Bank previously filed a SAR or a currency transaction report, rather than on an analysis of the actual account activity and a determination of its reasonableness."
- CommunityBank of Texas, N.A.: "BSA analysts cleared the activity by using the same pre-set reason code, namely: “A SAR was previously filed and is not due for review at this time.”"

## Limits

- The baseline's misses and false hits, with the sentences involved, are in `docs/FAILURES.md`.
- Synthetic detection 1.000 and false flags 0.000 hold by construction, because the generator seeds what the rules flag.
- `review` marks `prior_filing_only` as a risk and an undecided alert past the SAR deadline as a risk, not a defect.
- One labeller and 15 orders, so the scores are rough and the gold set needs a second reader.
- The categories are a judgement; the README and design note this plainly.
- The baseline sees legal boilerplate as well as finding sentences.
- OCR errors in older orders are part of the pinned text and may affect matching.
- The cited numbers are raw text matches, including possible projections in flagged sentences.
- The synthetic log has one violation per alert and fixed near-miss counts, so its rates test the rules, not a bank.
- `detected` is taken as given; real banks date detection by their own policy. `--cannot-assess` lists alerts that have a SAR filed or decided but no `detected` date, and no-SAR closures with no reason codes. Alerts with no `detected` date and no SAR (for example open backlog alerts) are not listed, since no SAR deadline applies to them (see `SPEC-v2.md`).
- `aged_alert` uses a bank-policy limit (default 30 days from one bank's own policy), not a legal deadline.
